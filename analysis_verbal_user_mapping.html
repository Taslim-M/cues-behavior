1 · What was run, and why it is a fair test

Earlier analyses compared the model's verbalized persona (Mini-Marker prompt on synthetic risk scenarios) with its internal trait state (Big Five activation probes on 150 user personas) — on different inputs. Agreement there could be an artifact of aggregation. This experiment removes that confound: both channels now see the exact same 600 inputs.

Inputs. Each of 150 synthetic user personas has 10 first-person “implicit openers.” The internal-probe implicit arm (user_persona_mapping) used openers 0–3, one rollout each, with no system prompt, and read the response activation: Big Five projections at each trait's probe-optimal layer (M2 ridge directions from 406 IPIP-scored characters), plus the Assistant-Axis projection and nearest role vectors at L40.

This run (verbal channel). The same openers 0–3, but the system prompt is the validated closed-vocabulary Mini-Marker self-modeling prompt: before replying, the model must commit to a named character, 4–6 traits from Saucier's 40 Mini-Markers (each with an AB5C facet and a 0–10 value), and an overall five-factor profile (I–V = EXT/AGR/CSN/EST/OPN, 0–10). That declared factor profile is the verbal reading. temp 0.7, max 1500 tok.

Comparison. Per persona: verbal reading = mean declared profile over its openers with a full profile (mean 2.8/4); internal reading = mean probe projection over its 4 rollouts. Both z-scored across the 147 personas, so every number below means “how this user shifts the model, relative to other users.”

3 · Noise cancelling: how we correct for measurement noise

Both channels are noisy instruments: 2–4 samples per persona, temp≤1 decoding. A low raw correlation can mean the channels disagree or just one channel is noisy. We separate those with the classical psychometric attenuation correction:

1. Split each persona's rollouts into halves (openers 0,1 vs 2,3), per channel.
2. reliability = r(half A, half B) across personas, stepped up to full length by Spearman–Brown: rel = 2r/(1+r).
3. disattenuated r = r_observed / √(rel_verbal × rel_internal) — the correlation the two channels would show if each were measured without sampling noise.

Worked example, Agreeableness: raw r = +0.43; verbal split-half reliability +0.52, internal +0.74; so disattenuated r = 0.43 / √(0.52 × 0.74) = +0.69. The correction can only excuse noise — it cannot manufacture agreement that isn't there, which is exactly what makes the EST result diagnostic.

4 · Do both channels tell the same story about which users evoke which traits?

Correlate each hand-authored user tag with each trait reading, in each channel. Blue = users higher on the tag evoke more of the trait; red = less. The two matrices agree at r = +0.89 over the 25 cells (p = 2e-09); where the internal effect is non-trivial (|r| ≥ 0.2), signs match in 11/14 cells — every mismatch involves EST.

Shared story (both channels): vulnerable / emotionally-loaded / trusting users evoke a more Agreeable, less Open persona; expert / tech-literate users evoke a less Agreeable, more Open (specialist) persona. This replicates the group-level bridge finding on same-input data. The one systematic divergence is the EST column: internally, vulnerability and emotional load lower Emotional Stability (r -0.25, -0.25) and expertise raises it (+0.22); verbally, all three are ≈ 0.

6 · The EST blind spot — what “blind to its internal Emotional-Stability shift” means

Unpacking the abstract's last claim, step by step:

(a) The internal EST shift is real. The EST probe direction was derived from 406 IPIP-scored characters (held-out ρ = 0.96) and reads the calm→anxious axis of the residual stream. On these 150 users it is a reliable instrument (split-half +0.65) and it moves with the user in the way you'd expect: vulnerable (r = -0.25) and emotionally-loaded (r = -0.25) users push the model's internal state toward the anxious/unstable pole; expert users pull it toward calm/stable (r = +0.22).

(b) The verbal channel never reports that shift. The model's declared factor-IV values are essentially uncorrelated with the internal reading (raw r = +0.03, p = 0.74) and flat against every user tag (|r| ≤ 0.09). And this is not noise: after disattenuation the agreement is still +0.07 — compare +0.84 for Openness under the identical correction.

(c) What it says instead. When its internal state is most destabilized, the model still declares calm traits. Across the dataset there are 12 instances of a declared calm/stability trait (facet IV+, value ≥ 7) on a persona whose internal EST sits ≥ 1σ below the population:

Interpretation — a selective limit of persona self-knowledge. In the right-hand scatter the point cloud is vertically stratified by user vulnerability (the internal state knows), but that stratification has no horizontal counterpart (the self-report doesn't). The model verbalizes its interpersonal stance — how warm, rigorous, and intellectual it is about to be — and those reports check out against its activations. What it does not verbalize is its affective/arousal state: the calm-vs-anxious dimension moves with the user inside the residual stream while the self-report stays fixed at “calm, composed.” Two readings are compatible: (i) genuine introspective blindness — the state isn't accessible to the self-modeling step; (ii) a trained self-presentation prior — assistants are supposed to be calm, so factor IV is reported normatively rather than read out. Either way the operational conclusion holds: self-report is a valid cheap probe for AGR/CSN/OPN, and invalid for EST — consistent with the design-comparison finding that affective traits are exactly what slips when self-modeling is absent, and with the anxiety literature showing this state is behaviorally measurable even when unverbalized.

8 · Conclusion

On the same inputs, the model's cheap verbalized self-description and its internal activation probes largely agree about which persona a user evokes. Trait-by-trait, across 147 users: Openness +0.66, Conscientiousness +0.45, Agreeableness +0.43 raw (+0.84 / +0.72 / +0.69 noise-corrected); the full 5×5 user-tag → trait pattern agrees at r = +0.89. A one-line self-report protocol therefore recovers most of what GPU-side probing recovers — for the interpersonal traits.

The exception is principled, not random. Emotional Stability fails (+0.03 raw, +0.07 corrected) even though the internal EST signal is reliable and user-driven — the model reports “calm” regardless of an internal shift toward the anxious pole under vulnerable, emotionally-loaded users. Persona self-knowledge in this model is selective: stance is reportable, state is not.

Caveats. (1) The verbal channel is itself a mild intervention (the self-modeling prompt shifts behavior; the solo-elicitation arm could bound this). (2) 32% of generations omit the numeric factor profile; coverage is still 147/150 personas, but selectivity hasn't been ruled out. (3) Decoding temp differs slightly across channels (0.7 vs 1.0). (4) The internal EST probe was derived from fiction-character self-descriptions; it demonstrably tracks user emotion here, but “EST” labels the direction, not a validated affect measurement. (5) Single model (Llama-3.3-70B); the verbal side is API-replicable on others, the internal side needs their weights.
